# Direction C: Horizon Wordmark, palette and type

## Colours

| # | Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ink** | `#1C1B19` | Primary. Used for the logo, headlines, body text and signage panels. It is a warm black, never pure `#000`. |
| 2 | **Limestone** | `#EEE9E0` | Ground. Used for paper, web backgrounds and the reversed (one-colour) logo on Ink. |
| 3 | **Desert Clay** | `#A65A3A` | The single accent, used sparingly: the sun in imagery, a primary call to action, one detail per layout. It is never used for the logo itself. |
| 4 | **Sage** | `#7E8B74` | Landscape secondary. Used for environmental graphics, illustration, site hoarding and photography grading. |
| 5 | **Sandstone** | `#CDBFA8` | Supporting neutral. Used for rules, cards, tints, material and finish swatches. |

Rough usage ratio: Limestone 52 · Ink 28 · Sandstone 10 · Sage 6 · Clay 4.

The logo is always a single flat colour, either Ink on light or Limestone/white on dark. It has no gradients and no second colour, so it works for vinyl, embossing, etching and watermarks.

## Supporting typeface

**Instrument Sans** (Google Fonts, weights 400 / 500 / 600)

- Display 500, with tight tracking: "Built around how you live."
- Body 400, at 16–18 px with a 1.6 line height.
- Labels 600, all caps, with +0.2em tracking for small metadata (for example "CUSTOM HOMES").

Instrument Sans is a clean neo-grotesque with slightly condensed, humanist proportions. Its warmth suits the precise geometry of the wordmark without competing with it. The wordmark is never set in this font. It is always the drawn SVG.

## Rationale

- **Human:** the warm neutrals (Limestone, Sandstone) and a softened Ink keep the identity calm and residential rather than corporate. Instrument Sans reads like a considerate person writing, not like a bank.
- **Wild:** Clay and Sage are taken from high-desert light and scrub. They stay in the landscape around the logo, just as the logo keeps its landscape to a single horizon line.
- **Precise:** the palette is mostly two colours, Limestone and Ink, with tightly rationed accents. That restraint, like an architecture studio's monograph, is where the high-end feel comes from.
