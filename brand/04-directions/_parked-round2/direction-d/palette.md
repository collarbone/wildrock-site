# Direction D: Ridge-Cut Wordmark (palette and type)

## Colours

| # | Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Graphite** | `#2B2926` | Primary. The wordmark, headlines, body text, yard-sign panels, favicon tile and dark grounds. A warm, slightly brown graphite (pencil lead, wet slate), never pure black. |
| 2 | **Oat** | `#ECE5D8` | Ground. Paper, web backgrounds, and the reversed wordmark on Graphite. Warmer and more yellow than a plain stone white. |
| 3 | **Oxide** | `#9A3F2B` | The single accent: iron oxide in canyon rock. One use per layout (a CTA, a rule, a sign's descriptor band). It is never used for the wordmark. |
| 4 | **Pumice** | `#C8BFB0` | Supporting neutral. Cards, rules, landscape layers, material swatches, tints. |
| 5 | **Flint** | `#645E57` | Secondary text, captions, metadata and drafting linework. Passes AA on Oat at body sizes (5.1:1). |

Rough usage: Oat 55 · Graphite 25 · Pumice 10 · Flint 7 · Oxide 3.

The wordmark only ever appears in one flat colour: Graphite on light, Oat or white (`#FFFFFF`) on dark and on photography. No gradients, no second colour, no outline version. This keeps it ready for vinyl, routed signage, embossing, etching and watermarks.

## Supporting typeface

**Archivo** (Google Fonts, variable: `wdth` 62–125, `wght` 100–900).

- Labels and descriptors: width 125, weight 500–600, all caps, +0.3–0.4em tracking ("CUSTOM HOMES").
- Headlines: width 100–112, weight 500, tight tracking: "Built around how you live."
- Body: width 100, weight 400, 16–18 px, 1.6 line height.

The wordmark is drawn from the same family, so the system feels like one studio's hand. The wordmark itself is always the outlined SVG, never live type, because the ridge cut and tuned spacing only exist in the drawing.

## Rationale

- **Human:** Oat and Pumice are soft, warm, domestic neutrals (linen, plaster, unbleached paper). Nothing is bright white or pure black, so the identity feels residential rather than corporate.
- **Wild:** Oxide is the colour of iron-stained canyon walls and high-desert rock. It is used as sparingly as it appears in a landscape: a single seam of colour.
- **Precise:** Graphite and Flint come from the drafting table: pencil lead and construction lines. Paired with lots of Oat, they give the quiet, editorial restraint of an architecture studio.
- **Distinct from other directions:** warmer, yellower ground (Oat rather than Limestone or Bone), a redder and darker accent (Oxide rather than Clay), and no green.
